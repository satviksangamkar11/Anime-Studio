## 2. The rethink: three locks, and H3 as the renderer
Both audits treat the text prompt as the main way to get motion. That is the weak link: our own clip shows H3 barely follows a text action. The tutorials point the other way:

| Lock | Comes from | Not from |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity** | Flow reference family (identity, turnaround, expressions) and keyframes made FROM it; Ref2VA references where the shot needs them | words |
| **Motion and camera** | a **driving video**: (a) a person acting the shot on a phone (Mickmumpitz "follow acting"), (b) a Blender layout with simple mannequins and the real camera move, turned into a pose or depth video for H3 Control | a list of actions in the prompt |
| **Style** | the keyframes (H3 image-to-video keeps the picture's look) + the style bible text | film names |

So H3 becomes the **renderer** that paints our characters, in our style, onto motion and camera that we designed. Text prompts only describe small things (expression, cloth, light, sound).

This also answers the hardest parts of the audits:
- **Choreography** (Krishna's leap, Kaliya's strike, the tandava): block it in Blender or act it; render pose/depth; H3 Control follows it. Real distances, contact and momentum, not guessed.
- **Camera** (push, crane, orbit, parallax): exact, from the Blender camera, instead of hoping the model obeys "static camera".
- **Dialogue**: ElevenLabs voice first; H3 keeps that soundtrack and moves the lips (the mode "pinned to a voice line" in Mickmumpitz; Wan2GP `audio_prompt_type` A for FL2VA, AS for Ref2VA). No layer cut-outs needed.
- **Anime timing**: holds on 2s/3s and impact frames in post, from an exposure sheet per shot; camera and effects stay at 24 fps.

## 3. The pipeline (per story)
1. **Write** (built): staged writer, any model. Gate 3 adds per shot: `route`, `motion_source` (none / act / blender), integer `render_frames` and `edit_frames`.
2. **Art in Flow** (owner's work, guided by the pack): per character an identity sheet, a turnaround, and an expression sheet; environments; then each shot's start keyframe made FROM the sheets (and the end keyframe as an edit of the start). Watermark removal. A quick identity check against the sheet before any GPU time.
3. **Motion**: for each shot with `motion_source`:
   - *act*: film it on a phone against a plain wall (any cast member, the owner, a friend), 24 fps, same framing as the shot;
   - *blender*: a mannequin layout + camera, rendered as depth (and a pose video by running a pose detector on the mannequin render).
4. **Render on Kaggle** (Wan2GP, one queue per session, PNG inputs, models cached):

| Route | Use | Wan2GP model |
|---|---|---|
| R1 plain | simple shots: small acting, holds that need life, environment shots | FL2VA pruned + Pika 6-step (proven) |
| R2 identity | recurring characters in new scenes, close-ups | Ref2VA pruned + Pika 6-step, sheet as `<Picture 1>`, `retention_analysis` |
| R3 motion | choreography, jumps, fights, creature motion, exact camera | H3 Control pruned (pose or depth) + start/end keyframes |
| R4 dialogue | lines on screen | FL2VA or Ref2VA with the ElevenLabs line as kept soundtrack |
| POST | still holds, impact frames, captions, grade | ffmpeg / Python, no GPU |

5. **Finish**: second T4 runs the anime upscaler / interpolation while the first generates; post adds exposure holds, impact frames, captions, colour; ElevenLabs music/SFX; edit.
6. **Accept**: automatic checks (frame count, camera drift where the camera should be fixed, flicker, end-frame match), then the owner watches the finished clip; identity, anatomy or canon errors reject.
