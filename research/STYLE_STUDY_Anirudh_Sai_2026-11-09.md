# Anirudh and Sai Abhyankkar Style Study

Nov 9, 2026 · @Sangeeta

## Bottom line

Both composers build scores from the same three parts: a short, chantable hook that becomes the character's identity, a hard electronic beat (techno, trap, EDM pulse), and one live Indian sound on top (folk drums, nadaswaram, chant, flute). Anirudh is the master of the **elevation BGM**: a long build that lands exactly on the hero's big moment. Sai Abhyankkar pushes harder into **club and folk-electronic**, and his Karuppu score (May 2026) shows how a deity gets its own sound: a recurring motif whenever the god's idol is on screen (its instruments are not confirmed), then a full folk-electronic burst in the action.

What we take for the Kalika reels: a chant hook per deity, techno and DnB pulses on a 60/128/176 BPM grid so every cut still lands on a beat, minimal tension that bursts at the reveals, and a mix that never buries the voice. What we avoid: the same loud texture for 70 s, a theme repeated until it dies, and mixes that fight the dialogue, which are exactly what critics attacked in Coolie, The Paradise, Dude and Karuppu.

This is a study of their techniques only. We write original hooks and sounds; no melody, chant or sample from their work is reused.

## Sai Abhyankkar's toolkit

He is 21, produces, programs and mixes his own work, and came from viral indie singles into the biggest films within two years ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sai_Abhyankkar), [Dude credits](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dude_(soundtrack)>)).

| Technique | Where it shows | How we use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Club production with pop hooks**, intricate under the surface ([Rolling Stone India](https://rollingstoneindia.com/future-of-music-2025/sai-abhyankkar/)) | Katchi Sera (250M+ views) | Kamadeva's playful section: a groove you could dance to, hook on top |
| **Tamil folk feel on a dance-floor beat** ([Rolling Stone India, via Magzter](https://magzter.com/de/stories/entertainment/RollingStone-India/SAI-ABHYANKKAR-PLANS-MORE-RELEASES-AND-FILM-MUSIC-AFTER-KATCHI-SERA-SUCCESS)) | Katchi Sera | Folk-style percussion pattern over a techno kick |
| **Live Indian players over his synths**: sarangi, nadaswaram, live rhythm group | Dude | Reserve one live-sounding solo (bowed or reed) for emotional peaks |
| **One song as the film's emotional motif**, in an unplugged version | Dude (Oorum Blood) | A stripped piano/vocal version of the main theme for the cliffhanger |

**God scoring in Karuppu (the most useful film for us):** the article on its music describes four moves ([Sacnilk](https://legacy.sacnilk.com/news/karuppu-box-office-sai-abhyankkars-music-and-the-god-mode-anthem-spark-a-spiritual-cultural-phenomenon-in-theaters)):

1. A **fixed deity motif**: a motif recurs whenever the camera is on the Karuppasamy idol, so the god is felt through the whole film (one article; its instruments are not confirmed).
2. **Restraint first**: not confirmed. One article described quiet courtroom scoring before the burst, but a fresh check found no source for it, and one review calls the whole score loud. We keep restraint-then-release as our own rule, not as a fact about Karuppu.
3. **Then the burst**: the "God Mode" theme explodes in the action.
4. **Folk rhythm + electronics = temple festival**: heavy drums and modern electronic sounds together. The article reports audiences going into trance states during those sequences, which shows how strong repetitive folk-electronic rhythm is for divine moments.

For Kalika this maps directly: Kali and Shiva each get a fixed motif (chant + one signature instrument), held back under the story, then released once per reel.

## What critics complained about

The same five complaints come up across their 2025-2026 films. Each becomes a rule or an automatic check in our system.

| Complaint | Films | Our rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Loud all the time; exhausting | Karuppu ([Gulte](https://www.gulte.com/moviereviews/411501/karuppu-veerabhadrudu-movie-review/amp)), The Paradise | Intensity curve per act; full power only at the one elevation per reel |
| Repetitive, deja vu, predictable signature sound | Coolie ([Gulte](https://www.gulte.com/moviereviews/366633/coolie-movie-review/amp)), Karuppu, Anirudh in general ([Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/tabloid/hyderabad-chronicle/is-anirudh-losing-his-midas-touch-1992830)) | Themes transform (tempo, harmony, instrument) instead of repeating; a new layer every shot |
| Score overpowers the picture | Dude ([Cinema Express, via Wikipedia](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dude_(soundtrack)>)) | Hits only on planned sync points from shots.json, never on every expression |
| Bad sound mixing; lyrics or dialogue buried | The Paradise ([Jago News](https://www.jagonews24.com/en/entertainment/news/96396)), Karuppu | Voice-window check: score thin at 1-4 kHz under every line; Gate D ducking |
| No emotional poignancy, only mass | Karuppu ([Popcorn Reviewss](https://popcornreviewss.com/karuppu-2026-movie-review/)) | Every reel has at least one quiet, melodic moment (an unplugged theme) |

## Pattern library from the score albums

Leo, Kingdom, Jana Nayagan, The Paradise and Jailer 2 have no score album I could find, so they appear only through reviews. Nothing here was listened to: it is what track lists, credits, sheet-music listings and reviews say. A fresh agent re-checked 22 of these claims: 16 confirmed, 4 partly confirmed, 2 not found. The corrections are applied in this doc.

| Cue type | What they do | Evidence | Our version for Kalika |

| Hero / divine entry | A short theme (under 1:30) built on the title motif, often over slow motion | JD Intro 1:20 opens Master; Rolex Theme 0:53 over Rolex's arrival; Deva Entry 0:44 (Coolie); Mathew's slow-motion entry in Jailer | Shiva at 48 s and Kali at 0 s get their theme as an 8-bar entry cue |
| One theme per character | Every main character is a named cue | Coolie (Anirudh: a special BGM for every character), Jailer (Mathew, Narasimha, Varman), Master (JD, Bhavani) | Already in our bible: Kali, Brahma, Sandhya, Kamadeva, Shiva |
| Interval / twist set piece | A dedicated cue for the big turn | Ghost Unmasked at Vikram's interval; Pre Interval and Interval Fight in Madharaasi; Karuppu's interval called "God Mode begins" by fans; Dude's interval is a rework of Oorum Blood | Each reel's mid-point re-hook (34 s) and the cliffhanger get their own cue |
| Fights from song material | Fight cues are mash-ups of the film's themes | Coolie: Powerhouse x Disco, Bus Fight x Powerhouse x Disco | The chaos at 41 s is built from Kamadeva's hook + the main theme's rhythm |

| Deity signature | A fixed sound whenever the god is on screen; restraint then release | Karuppu: a recurring motif on the idol; folk drums with electronics in God Mode (the quiet-then-burst structure is not confirmed) | Kali and Shiva each get a fixed signature sound, held back, released once per reel |
| Voice and odd sources as instruments | Signature sounds made from voices | Hukum's war horn is Anirudh's processed voice; Vikram's Moaning Theme is built from moans | Our sonic logo: a processed voice, recorded, never sampled from anyone's track |

## Our version: electronic, for the Kalika reels

The tempo grid is 60 / 128 / 176 BPM (final, 2026-11-09). Production guides put hypnotic techno examples at 131 to 135, melodic techno at 122 to 130 and drum and bass at 170 to 176 BPM, so the earlier 120/180 grid was off-genre. The cue sheet's tempo solver now picks each act's BPM inside the genre range, so every cut in the act and the act end land within one video frame (42 ms) of an 8th note. Tempo changes only at act changes. For Kalika Reel 1 that gives 128 BPM for BUILD (11 to 34 s; 124 also fits) and 176 BPM for DROP (34 to 64 s; the only drum and bass value that fits). The earlier 172 at 41 s is dropped: 41 s is not an act change, so the chaos there comes from density at the same 176. The re-hook moves to 34.0 s, inside the retention window (31.5 to 42 s).

**Genres, used as moods:**

| Genre | What defines it | Typical BPM (guides) | Our BPM | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hypnotic techno | One short loop that barely changes, slow filter and reverb moves, four-on-the-floor kick | examples at 131 to 135 | 128 | Narration-heavy frame story, suspense |
| Melodic techno / progressive | Wide pads, layered kick, arpeggios, long builds | 122 to 130 | 128 | Creation, wonder, Sandhya |
| Club + folk groove (the Sai Abhyankkar move) | Dance kick + Tamil folk percussion (kuthu is in 6/8) + pop hook |  | 128 | Kamadeva, the playful build into the re-hook |
| Drum and bass (liquid / neuro) | Breakbeats, kick on beats 1 and 3, rolling reese bass | 170 to 176 (neuro up to 180) | 176 | Chaos and chase |
| Half-time DnB | Drums at DnB speed, bass and chords at half time (feels about 88 at 176) | 170 to 174 | 176 (feels 88) | Shiva, the curse: the elevation |
| Ambient / unplugged | Drone, piano or voice alone |  | 60 | Kali's awe, the cliffhanger |

**What this needs in Ableton:** Serum covers basses (reese, sub, distorted), supersaws, plucks and arps. Drums come from an Ableton Drum Rack with your own or royalty-free samples. Chants and vocal horns need recorded voice (yours or a cleared vocal pack); I would not generate or copy them.

**Mix for phones, not theatres:** their scores are built for cinema sub-bass. Ours keeps a mid harmonic on every bass, mono-safe drums, and stays thin under every voice line.

**Suggested experiment:** build the same 0-18 s test twice, A = the modern cinematic plan already in the scoring doc, B = this electronic plan. You listen to both against the picture at gate M1 and pick, or ask for a mix of the two.
