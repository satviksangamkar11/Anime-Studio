## a) Story spine

**Protagonist:** Sati. She is the Goddess Mahamaya (Yoganidra, the dark four-armed Kali form) who chooses to be born as Daksha's daughter, and she returns as Parvati.

**Want:** "She wants to win the ascetic Shiva as her husband, so that creation can go on."

**Flaw / inner tension:** "Her love is fierce but proud: she binds herself with the vow 'If you disrespect me, I will leave this body at once', and she cannot bend it even for her father." Under the pride sits a shyness that stops her from saying what she wants.

**Crisis:** "When Daksha shuts Shiva and her out of his yajna as 'the skull-bearer's wife', her own anger and her own vow leave her no road back, and she gives up her body by yoga."

**Antagonistic pressure:** "Daksha is no flat villain: he did hard tapasya to have her as his daughter and gave her to Shiva with joy, but pride in ritual purity turns him against the son-in-law who carries skulls." A second pressure is Shiva himself, pulled between yoga and attachment, then between grief and his duty to the world.

**Setup (Episodes 1–2, the problem she will solve):** Brahma creates Kamadeva. His flower arrows stir Brahma and the sages over Sandhya. This is shown with restraint, as shame and rebuke only. Shiva rebukes them and Brahma curses Kamadeva: "Shiva's eye will burn you." Kamadeva, Rati and Vasant then fail to move Shiva, and Brahma admits: "No one but Mahamaya can move him."

**Turning points:**
1. "At Daksha's tapasya she promises to be born his daughter and Shiva's wife, and she sets her own condition." (Episode 3)
2. "At her vrat Shiva stands before her, but shyness stops her tongue; once Kamadeva's arrow strikes them both and Shiva says 'Be my wife again', she still makes him ask her father properly." (Episode 5)
3. "Daksha's words turn from 'My daughter is meant for Shiva alone' to 'He carries skulls; he has no place at my yajna'." (Episodes 6–8)
4. "Before she leaves her body she chooses her own return, as the daughter of Himavan and Mena." Shiva's grief then carries her body across the sky. Vishnu's Sudarshan chakra separates it, and lights fall where the Shakti Peethas rise. (Episodes 9–11)
5. "As Parvati she serves Shiva quietly, but when Kamadeva's flower arrow is burnt by the third eye, she learns that beauty and service cannot win him." (Episode 12)

**Resolution:** "Shiva comes to her because of her vrat, and the last union is won by Parvati's own tapasya after Kamadeva's arrow has turned to ash." Shiva tests her as a brahmachari, accepts her in his true form, and they marry. Kartikeya is born and Tarakasura is slain.

**Ending anchor:** "The ending reuses Sati's night-long riverbank vigil with bilva leaves and the boon she could not voice: Parvati keeps the same vigil, alone and without any arrow, and this time she answers Shiva herself." The repeated vigil image is VISUAL_STAGING. Parvati's tapasya and the test are CANON as told in this telling.

**Canon flags (kept, not silently resolved):**
- **Sati's death:** Part 2's detailed telling is used. She hears of the insult, leaves her body by yoga at home, and Vijaya witnesses it. Part 1's recap differs: there she goes to the yajna and burns in yogic fire.
- **The destruction of the yajna:** Part 1's recap has Virabhadra and Mahakali born from Shiva's jata, and Daksha beheaded. That version is flagged as a variation. The yajna's destruction is shown without gore.
- **Order of events:** in Part 1's recap Kamadeva is burnt before Parvati is born. Part 2's order (born, then serving Shiva, then Kamadeva burnt) is used.
- **The Sudarshan chakra:** this is the speaker's telling. The classical Kalika Purana has the gods enter the body. This is marked UNVERIFIED for the owner. The body is shown only as falling lights.
- **The ending:** Kartikeya and Tarakasura appear only in summary form and stay brief.
